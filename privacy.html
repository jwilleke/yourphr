Privacy Policy

Last updated: 31 July 2026

YourPHR is self-hosted software. Your health records live on the server you (or your instance operator) run. The YourPHR open-source project and yourphr.org do not receive, store, sell, or have access to your medical or Medicare claims data. There is no YourPHR multi-tenant cloud that holds enrollees’ records.

What data is involved

On an instance, YourPHR can store:

  • Health information you import (FHIR records, documents you upload). For Medicare via CMS Blue Button, that is typically claims-related data such as Patient, Coverage, and ExplanationOfBenefit.
  • Account data for the instance (e.g. username and password hash — never your Medicare.gov password).
  • Connection data: provider settings and encrypted OAuth tokens used only to fetch data you authorized.

By default the database is designed to be encrypted at rest when encryption is enabled and working. The operator controls keys, backups, and who can log in.

The project maintainers do not collect your health records, Medicare claims, or provider tokens.

How Medicare (CMS Blue Button) data is collected

When you choose to connect Medicare through the CMS Blue Button API:

  • You sign in with Medicare / CMS. YourPHR never asks for your Medicare.gov password.
  • You authorize read-only access to the data scopes the app requests.
  • CMS returns a short-lived authorization code. A public sign-in relay may hold that code in memory for about 60 seconds so your instance can retrieve it. The relay never receives access or refresh tokens and never sees health data. Your instance exchanges the code for tokens directly with CMS.
  • Tokens are stored encrypted on the instance and used only to download the data you authorized.
  • Imported data is stored and displayed only on that instance (and any backups the operator makes).

Sharing with CMS: only what OAuth and the API require. Sharing with the YourPHR project: none of your Medicare data.

How long collection continues: the instance may re-sync while the connection remains authorized and tokens work. You can disconnect the source at any time. Disconnect removes stored credentials for that source; previously imported records remain until you or the operator delete them (unless the operator’s policy says otherwise).

How data is used

On the instance, data is used only to help you view and organize your own health information (and other users the operator allows).

YourPHR is not designed to sell your data, use it for advertising or third-party marketing, or train commercial AI models on your records.

De-identified data: the stock software does not package your Medicare data for sale or research as de-identified datasets. Even “anonymized” health data can sometimes re-identify people; we do not claim zero risk for any real-world dataset.

Third parties

  • CMS / Medicare — you authorize; they provide claims-related data under their rules.
  • Sign-in relay — authorization code only (~60 seconds); no health data, no tokens.
  • Operator infrastructure (hosting, reverse proxy, SSO) — must be secured by the operator; should not be used as a secondary store of claim payloads.
  • GitHub Pages (this website only) — serves static pages; no health data.

Operators should ensure any vendor that handles personal information meets legal and contractual expectations for the sensitivity of that information.

Your control and deletion

  • You can choose not to connect Medicare or any provider.
  • You can disconnect a source (removes stored OAuth credentials for that source).
  • You can delete records or your account where the product provides those features, or ask the operator to wipe the deployment and backups.

If you revoke access at Medicare/CMS, new fetches may stop. Data already on the instance stays until deleted there.

Dormant or closed accounts: data remains on the operator’s disk until the account, database, or backups are removed. The project holds no dormant enrollee databases.

Security

The software aims to reduce risk: local storage, at-rest encryption when active, no project-side copy of records, short-lived relay codes. Operators must also use HTTPS, strong access control, secure backups, and current software. No system is perfect.

Breach notification

If a breach affects health information on an instance, the instance operator is responsible for assessing the incident and notifying people as required by law (including, where applicable, the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule for personal health records, and other federal or state rules).

The YourPHR project does not hold your Medicare data and cannot notify you of a breach of your instance. If project-operated infrastructure (for example the public relay or this website) is involved in an incident that could affect users, we will communicate appropriately (for example GitHub security notices or a site notice).

If the project or operator is sold

A change in open-source maintainers does not move your instance’s database. If the operator of a hosted instance is sold or changes how data is used, that operator must notify you when the law or their commitments require it; you should be able to disconnect Medicare and delete or export your data.

This website (yourphr.org)

Static informational site. No health information. No first-party analytics cookies. The host may log standard request metadata (such as IP address).

If you operate an instance for others

You are the operator/controller of that deployment. You are responsible for user privacy, access control, backups, and regulations that apply to you (for example HIPAA if you are a covered entity). This policy describes the software’s default behaviour, not a multi-tenant service run by the project.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy. The “Last updated” date will change at this URL. For a CMS-approved Blue Button production application, draft policy changes and enrollee notices may need CMS review before rollout (see CMS production-access guidance).
